Research & Publications

PASTA: A Scalable Framework for Multi-Policy AI Compliance Evaluation
CHI 2026

AI practitioners, such as developers, UI/UX designers, and product managers, face a growing number of policies, each with complex requirements that are difficult to interpret without legal expertise.

We developed PASTA, an open-source, LLM-powered tool that helps developers assess their AI systems against multiple policies. Given a description of an AI system, PASTA identifies requirements that are met or potentially unmet and provides actionable recommendations for improvement. Our goal is to make policy compliance more accessible to everyday AI practitioners.

Negotiating Robot Autonomy to Preserve User Responsiveness: A User Enactment Study of Personal Security Robots for Women's Safety
CHI 2027 Under Review

A woman is walking home alone when a stranger starts bothering her. A personal security robot could step in to help, but what if she doesn't want it to? Or what if it does nothing when she really needs help?

We wanted to explore how robots could protect women without taking away their control over the situation. We invited 15 women and female-identifying participants to act out six everyday safety scenarios with a researcher playing the robot. Through these enactments, we explored when women wanted a robot to step in, when they preferred to handle things themselves, and how a robot might appropriately act.

Experience

Software Engineering Intern, Optum (Sept 2024 – Apr 2025)

I worked on internal infrastructure at Optum after a period of corporate restructuring and database migration. Many operational applications were originally designed to communicate with local database servers, where network latency was negligible. When the databases migrated, frequent and inefficient SQL queries became a major bottleneck.

One of these applications was Traceability Matrix, an internal system for managing project information, development sprints, tickets, and product records. It had been out of service for nearly a year, and its original developers had left the company. My work involved restoring the application and refactoring its C# code and SQL stored procedures to accommodate the new database infrastructure. The restored system supports:

  • Development tracking: Synchronizing and organizing project, sprint, and ticket records.
  • Product traceability: Linking product information to related projects, sprints, and tickets.
  • Historical records: Retrieving past development activities and product documentation.
  • Project overview: Providing a centralized view of development activities across projects.
